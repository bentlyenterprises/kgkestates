This area is alive with gatherings that celebrate rural life, heritage, and shared rhythms of the seasons. Many community events here are handed down for generations. This is where neighbours and visitors mix easily, and the community’s character shines most bright..

Kildrummy Veteran & Vintage Vehicles Club Rally

Every summer, the Kildrummy Rally draws enthusiasts and curious visitors alike to admire classic cars, vintage tractors, and motorcycles. Held on the estate grounds, it’s a relaxed, family-friendly event that feels part country fete, part living museum. Engines hum, stories are swapped, and the patch of land becomes a home for mechanical nostalgia.

Aboyne Highland Games

A short drive away, the Aboyne Games is a spring fixture dating back to 1867. Strong athletes weave through caber tosses while pipe bands set the tempo. There’s dancing, children’s races, and highland hospitality under the open sky.

Aberdeen Highland Games

Held at Hazlehead Park in Aberdeen in the summertime, this event draws up to 15,000 people for heavy athletics, Highland dancing, piping competitions, and local trade stalls. Meanwhile, families gather on grassy banks to soak in the spectacle of kilts and camaraderie.

Alford Heritage Museum Spring Fair

Each spring, Alford Heritage Museum hosts its Spring Fair to celebrate local crafts, fresh produce, and community connection. It’s a chance to meet makers, learn about regional traditions, and enjoy seasonal treats in a welcoming, low-key setting.

Huntly Hairst (Harvest Festival)

Every September, Huntly town square overflows with stalls selling artisan food, crafts, and produce. The Hairst Week wraps up on Hairst Day, which features farmers’ market stalls, cookery demos, a “Hairst Hoolie” ceilidh, kids’ activities, and the quirky World Stovies Championship.

Beyond these, other gatherings reflect the wider spirit of Aberdeenshire. The Ballater Duck Festival in June celebrates spring with rubber ducks and riverside entertainment. Another crowd-drawing event is Spectra, Aberdeen’s winter festival of light, which transforms public spaces with projection art and illuminated installations.

There are also seasonal highlights like Taste of Grampian and Provenance festivals, both celebrating local food and drink.

These celebrations are markers on our calendar, reminders of culture, land, and community. Whether you’re stopping by to watch vintage engines idle, test your strength at the Highland Games, or browse craft stalls under the autumn sun, you’ll find kinship, nostalgia, and local pride. All are welcome here.

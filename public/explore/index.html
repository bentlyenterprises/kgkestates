The scenic views of the Kildrummy and Glenkindie Estates are just the introduction of what awaits in our neck of the woods. Tucked between the eastern edges of the Cairngorms and the quiet bends of the River Don, these lands hold history that lingers in castle stones and on rolling hills. Here, the forest paths lead to unexpected adventures and local flavors make every visit memorable. We invite you to hike, linger over tea, or follow in royal footsteps on centuries-old grounds.

Where to Eat, Drink, and Unwind

Aberdeenshire’s beauty extends well beyond the hills – it’s also found in its kitchens, pubs, and tearooms. The area surrounding the estates offers everything from fine dining to hearty pub fare, with warm welcomes and thoughtful touches along the way.

In the village of Alford, The Alford Bistro is a local favourite known for its seasonal menus, friendly service, and relaxed café atmosphere. For those venturing west toward Strathdon, Goodbrand and Ross is more than just a café stop. Its shop and tearoom overlook sweeping views and serve up fresh baking alongside local crafts.

For a pint and a generous plate, the Steading Bar in Strathdon keeps things traditional, with a welcoming pub feel and regular community events. Not far away, The Commercial Hotel in Tarland pairs historic essence with modern comfort, and is particularly popular among dog owners who enjoy a place where all, four-legged friends included, are welcome.

In the heart of Aboyne, The Airedale gives guests a contemporary twist on pub classics. Just fifteen minutes from there you’ll find Ballater, where we recommend visiting The Craigendarroch Hotel. The elegant dining here is matched by views of the surrounding woodland – ideal for a special night out or a relaxed lunch during a day of sightseeing.

Wherever you go for a meal, expect warm service, a good dram, and the kind of hospitality that keeps you coming back.

A Bite to Go

Not every great meal needs a table. For cosy nights in or quick bites between adventures, the area’s takeaway options deliver flavour without fuss.

In Alford, Frank’s Fryer keeps things classic with generous portions of fresh fish and chips, while Alford Tandoori serves up aromatic curries and tandoori dishes packed with spice and depth. For those leaning east, the Great Wall Chinese offers familiar favourites in a no-frills style, perfect for a quiet evening at the cottage.

Ballater adds its own international flair, with New Shanghai Takeaway delivering satisfying Chinese staples and the Ballater Chip Shop keeping locals and visitors well-fed with traditional dishes. Two standout Indian options – Lochnagar Indian Brasserie and India on the Green – both offer a refined yet accessible menu of curries and regional dishes, with India on the Green especially known for its locally sourced ingredients and creative specials.

Wherever your cravings lead, the area’s takeaway spots have comfort, convenience, and a warm welcome, even when you’re dining fireside back at home.

Adventure for Every Season

There’s a spirit of movement woven into the land here. Whether you’re drawn to the mountains or prefer the quiet of a garden path, the Upper Don region is a space to roam, to try something new, and to reconnect with nature. Find snow-covered slopes, forested trails, and hands-on experiences with Rlocal wildlife, all just a short drive from our estates.

At Strathdon Ranch, visitors can enjoy guided trail rides through heathered hills, meet Highland cattle and Clydesdale horses, and experience the rhythms of rural life up close. The ranch’s hands-on approach makes it a popular stop for families and animal lovers alike.

Come winter, The Lecht 2090 turns into a hub for snow sports in the Eastern Cairngorms. With slopes for skiing and snowboarding, plus lessons for beginners and equipment hire on-site, it’s a welcoming and accessible alpine destination. In the warmer months, the area is just as appealing for hillwalking and mountain biking.

For something slower-paced, the Upper Donside Bowling Club is a scenic spot for a friendly match or a quiet afternoon of spectatorship. It’s a longstanding social anchor in the community, and visitors are always made to feel at home.

Cyclists and trail runners should make time for Tarland Trails, a well-maintained network of off-road paths ranging from beginner loops to more technical routes. Designed with both families and professionals in mind, it’s a fantastic way to explore the landscape under your own power.

More structured outdoor fun can be found at Deeside Activity Park, which has everything from quad biking and kart racing to archery and clay pigeon shooting. It’s a go-to destination for mixed-age groups or anyone looking for a little adrenaline between castle tours and garden walks.

And at Lochter Activity Centre, just outside Inverurie, you’ll find an equally broad mix of activities – fishing ponds, segway trails, off-road driving, and more – plus a popular café that makes it easy to turn a visit into a full-day outing.

However you like to spend your time, this part of Aberdeenshire serves a generous helping of the outdoors, seasoned with just the right touch of adventure.

Stone, Story, and Sovereignty

The castles of Aberdeenshire are storytellers in their own right. Each one bears the marks of battles, royal visits, architectural ambition, and changing eras. For visitors, they are not just beautiful backdrops but vivid entry points into Scotland’s layered past.

Just a short journey from the estates, Kildrummy Castle rises in partial ruin but full majesty. Once one of the most formidable strongholds in northern Scotland, it was the seat of the Earls of Mar and a key player in the Wars of Scottish Independence. Today, its atmospheric remains are cared for by Historic Environment Scotland and invite exploration through ancient passageways and views across the hills

Balmoral Castle, perhaps the most famous of them all, serves as the Scottish holiday home of the Royal Family. Set within a vast estate of forests, rivers, and gardens, the castle opens seasonally to the public. Visitors can explore the Ballroom and exhibitions, stroll through the manicured grounds, and glimpse the landscape that so enchanted Queen Victoria and Prince Albert.

Finally, Craigievar Castle is something out of a storybook. With its rose-hued stone and fairy-tale turrets, it’s one of the best-preserved examples of Scottish Baronial architecture. Managed by the National Trust for Scotland, the castle’s interiors remain largely unchanged since the Victorian era, a rare and intimate look at 17th-century life.

Museums that Move You

In this part of Scotland, museums are less about glass cases and more about connection. They invite you to step into working histories – of transport, agriculture, craftsmanship, and innovation – that still shape daily life across the region. Each visit is a chance to slow down, listen closely, and see the familiar from a different angle.

Just down the road in Alford, the Grampian Transport Museum brings the story of motion to life. With a collection that includes steam cars, motorcycles, and futuristic prototypes, engineering meets imagination here. Seasonal exhibitions and outdoor displays keep things fresh, and younger visitors will enjoy hands-on features throughout the museum.

Nearby, the Alford Heritage Museum is housed in a former agricultural auction mart and paints a picture of rural life from centuries past. Through displays of tools, clothing, and community artifacts, it offers a heartfelt look at the trades, traditions, and people that once defined the region – and still do, in many ways.

A little further afield but well worth the journey, the Speyside Cooperage Visitor Centre in Craigellachie gives guests a behind-the-scenes look at an essential part of Scotland’s whisky heritage. Here, craftsmen shape and repair oak casks by hand, using traditional methods passed down through generations. The guided tours are immersive, and watching the coopers at work is an experience all its own.

For a modern twist on learning, the Aberdeen Science Centre brings big ideas to life. With more than 60 interactive exhibits across topics like energy, health, space, and robotics, it’s a family favourite and a hub of curiosity. Workshops and live demonstrations round out the visit, making it an ideal stop for school-age children and curious adults alike.

Simple Joys & Lasting Memories for the Whole Family

Family time here tends to unfold outdoors, unhurried and full of small discoveries. From farm parks to saltwater swims, the area offers places where children can explore, parents can exhale, and everyone can share in the kind of experiences that linger long after the day is done.

At Wynford Farm Park, younger visitors can meet farm animals up close, explore indoor and outdoor play areas, and enjoy daily activities that range from tractor rides to goat grooming. It’s a place where curious minds and muddy boots are always welcome.

Down on the coast, Stonehaven Open Air Swimming Pool offers a rare treat: a heated Olympic-sized saltwater pool under the open sky. Open seasonally, it’s a nostalgic and refreshing way to cool off, with fun sessions for families and a retro charm that draws regulars year after year.

Easter Anguston Farm is where play and purpose intersect. Operated by the social care charity VSA, it’s both a working farm and a community learning space. Children can meet animals, wander the sensory garden, and enjoy the play park, while adults can explore the café and learn about the farm’s mission to support people of all abilities.

There’s no single way to experience this place. Whether you’re drawn to the hills, the history, or the slow rhythm of village life, the landscape has a way of meeting you where you are. Take your time. See what stays with you.
